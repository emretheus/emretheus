Distillation

Reranking

retrieval · ranking 12 min

Depth
01 The question

If vector search already found the right document, why do production systems pay for a second pass?

02 The intuition

Finding is easy. Ordering is hard.

The first pass of a search system is built for speed. It compares your query against millions of precomputed summaries and returns the closest ones — approximately. That word matters: it is optimized to find, not to judge.

The problem is that “close in meaning” and “actually answers the question” are different things. A document can repeat your topic’s vocabulary and contain nothing useful.

The fix is a second stage. A cross-encoder A model that reads two texts together — query and document — and outputs a single relevance score. Slower, because nothing can be precomputed. takes the query and one candidate and reads them together, like a person opening the book. It is far more accurate and far more expensive, so you only run it on a shortlist.

Think of it as two librarians. The first one walks you to the right shelf in a second — approximate, but fast. The second opens a handful of books and checks the actual pages.

Deeper

Why not rerank everything? Because the cross-encoder needs one full model pass per candidate. A vector search over a million chunks is a lookup; a cross-encoder over a million chunks is a million model calls. So the architecture is always the same shape: cheap recall, expensive precision.

03 The math

Precomputed comparison vs joint reading.

The two stages differ in one structural way: when the query meets the document.

A bi-encoder (vector search) embeds the query and the document independently:

score(q, d) = cos(E(q), E(d))

Both sides were computed long before they met — the document side at index time, the query side at search time. That is what makes it fast, and also what makes it approximate: each side is a summary.

A cross-encoder scores them as one input:

score(q, d) = f(q ⊕ d)

The ⊕ is a concatenation. Because query and document are read together, the model can weigh individual words against each other — “does the document actually answer this?” — but nothing can be precomputed.

Math

For a shortlist of k candidates, the cost of reranking is k forward passes of the cross-encoder. Vector search over n chunks is, in comparison, roughly independent of model depth; that is why n can be millions while k stays around 25–100.

Reranker scores are only comparable within one query, within one model. Do not mix them across models or compare them to cosine values — they answer different questions on different scales.

When lexical and vector stages are combined, their scores are often fused by rank rather than value — reciprocal rank fusion is the common default:

RRF(d) = Σ 1 / (60 + rank_i(d))

Rank fusion avoids the impossible question of whether a BM25 score of 12.4 is “better” than a cosine of 0.83.

Deeper

Practical shortlist numbers come from budgets, not theory. 50 candidates with a small cross-encoder on GPU is a common starting point; 25 on CPU. The upper bound is latency, not quality — quality usually flattens long before cost does.

04 Real system

Two retrieval ways in, one small list out.

Deeper

When is a reranker not worth it? When candidates are already few (a small internal corpus), when queries are exact lookups (order numbers, error codes), or when latency is the product. Reranking is a precision tool for fuzzy questions over large corpora — applied everywhere else it is just a tax.

05 Where it breaks

A reranker fixes order — nothing else.

  • It inherits chunking damage. If the answer was cut in half at index time, the reranker sees half an answer and scores it honestly: badly.
  • It cannot rescue recall. If the right document is not in the shortlist, no reranker will find it. Bad K is a recall bug, not a ranking bug.
  • Domain mismatch. A cross-encoder trained on web text may not understand your legal or medical vocabulary. Rerankers are models; they have blind spots too.
  • Latency and cost. Every candidate is a model call. The bill and the delay both scale with the shortlist size.
  • Score worship. Reranker scores are relevance estimates, not truth. Thresholds copied from a blog post rarely survive contact with real data.
  • Diminishing returns. After roughly the first 50 candidates, extra shortlist mostly buys latency, not quality.

The honest summary: reranking is the cheapest large quality win in retrieval — after the boring work of chunking and recall is done properly.

06 One sentence

If you remember one thing

A reranker is a slower, more careful reader that looks at your query and each candidate together — and fixes the order the fast pass got approximately right.
